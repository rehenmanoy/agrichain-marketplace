# AgriChain: Board Questions

The hard questions a business owner, investor or board could ask, written from their side of the table. Prepared 2026-11-05 after going through the documents, the code and some outside sources. Back to the [Business Plan](Business-Plan.md).

> **How to use this file:** we answer one group at a time, in order. When a question is answered, write the short answer in its row and change the status to **Answered**. Decisions also go into [Business-Plan.md](Business-Plan.md).

## The 10 hardest questions

If these aren't answered, the meeting can end early. Start here.

| # | Question | Why it is so dangerous |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Why would a farmer leave their trader? | The trader's offer may be simpler than ours |
| A2 | How much more does a farmer really earn? | Our main promise has no real number behind it |
| A4 | A farmer harvests 300 kg and we sell 20 kg. What happens to the rest? | Small buyers don't match big harvests |

## A. Does anyone actually want this?

| # | Question they will ask | Why they will ask it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Why would a farmer leave their trader? | A trader can take the whole harvest and every quality in one go, often with cash the same day and sometimes with loans. We offer small orders and payment after delivery. **Draft answer (2026-11-06):** the farmer may get only ₹25 to ₹30/kg from a trader when the market rate is ₹39 (example figures); on AgriChain they get about the market rate. The farmer doesn't have to leave: AgriChain is an **extra channel**, so joining risks nothing. They sell what they can with us and the rest as usual. | Answered |
| A2 | How much more does a farmer earn, in rupees? | We say "earns more than the local market" but have measured nothing. **Draft answer (2026-11-06):** ₹39 to ₹42/kg instead of ₹25 to ₹30, so ₹9 to ₹17 more per kg. These are example figures, so real ones are needed from farmers. | Draft |
| A3 | Have you spoken to real farmers and buyers? How many? | Every assumption so far comes from us, not from them. **Status 2026-11-07:** nobody spoken to yet; the idea is based on the general situation of farmers. Must be fixed before the pitch. | Open |
| A4 | A farmer harvests 300 kg and we sell 20 kg. What happens to the other 280 kg? | Vegetables don't wait. If the farmer still needs the trader for most of it, why bother? **Answer (2026-11-06):** the rest goes through the farmer's usual channel. AgriChain is an extra channel, and as buyers and hotels join, more of the harvest moves to it and the farmer depends less on the trader. A warehouse is an option, not the main answer: 280 kg for 15 days costs ₹4,200 and doesn't sell the produce. | Answered |
| A5 | Why would a family stop buying from the shop nearby? | The shop is immediate, sells everything, and lets them pick by hand. **Draft answer (2026-11-07):** a good price, but we would have to compete with market traders. Weak: with a ₹39 tomato, 1 kg delivered costs ₹62.17 and 5 kg costs ₹46.17 per kg, so small orders aren't cheaper than a ₹45 shop price. | Draft |
| A6 | What do farmers around Erumely actually grow? | Sources disagree on how much of Kerala's vegetables are grown locally (about 22% to 75%). Our catalogue has 80 items, but a buyer also wants onion and potato, which may not be local. **Status 2026-11-07:** not known yet; the team doesn't know a farmer nearby. Must be fixed before the pitch. This also affects the pilot area (Business Plan Q1). | Open |
| A7 | Who is your first customer: homes or hotels? | A home buys 1 kg; a hotel buys 50 kg and wants daily supply. They need different products. **Answer (2026-11-07):** homes and small eateries first, while marketing to hotels. Homes build proof, not profit: after the gateway charge and damage fund, a 1 kg order leaves about ₹3.70. Eateries buy more of a harvest: 20 kg at ₹39 costs them ₹43.17/kg delivered (example fees). Big hotels follow once there are ratings and steady supply. | Answered |
| A8 | What happens in the monsoon and in the off-season? | When local supply stops, do buyers leave? **Answer (2026-11-07):** buyers may use the retail market in the off-season, and trust brings them back when farmers have stock. To be added: tell buyers when produce returns, and don't buy from traders to fill gaps, as that would make AgriChain a trader. | Draft |

## B. The money

| # | Question they will ask | Why they will ask it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | How much do you keep from one order? | On the ₹555 example we earn ₹20. The ₹1 damage fund and the gateway charge (about 2.36%, ₹13.10) leave about **₹5.90**, before the split fee, SMS and staff. **Draft answer (2026-11-07):** accept about ₹6 per order during the pilot and invest in it; do not raise fees, since farm-fresh food must stay affordable. Margin grows with more eateries, hotels and small vendors. Note: bigger orders barely help, as 20 kg keeps only about ₹7. **Added 2026-11-16:** eateries, hotels and restaurants pay a monthly fee, and home prices stay the same. | Draft |
| B2 | How many orders pay for one coordinator visit? | If a visit costs ₹100 (an example, not decided), that's about 17 orders. **Draft answer (2026-11-07):** visits aren't daily. After the first visit, farmers phone the coordinator with updates. Still to settle: paying coordinators for phone work (Q5). | Draft |
| B3 | What does it cost to bring in one farmer? | Akshaya fee, eKYC, coordinator visit, admin time. All paid by us. | Open |
| B4 | How many orders a day to break even? | We have never calculated it. Example only: ₹30,000 a month of costs at ₹6 per order needs 5,000 orders a month, about 167 a day; 400 pilot orders bring about ₹2,400. **Draft answer (2026-11-07):** the pilot won't break even and that's accepted. Growth comes from more orders and more business buyers. Real costs still needed. | Draft |
| B5 | How much money do you want, for what share, and spent on what? | No figure exists yet. | Open |
| B6 | How big is this market? | They expect a number for Kerala and for your first district. | Open |
| B7 | Larger, funded companies struggle in fresh produce. Why will you do better? | WayCool, Ninjacart and DeHaat have reported heavy losses, and Otipy struggled too. | Open |
| B8 | Who pays for free first deliveries and free trial weeks? | Every free offer comes out of a very small margin. | Open |
| B9 | Hotels usually pay after 15 to 30 days. Who waits for that money? | Farmers want money at once. Someone has to carry the gap. | Open |
| B10 | How do you collect warehouse commission? | The payment plan covers orders only. Booking money isn't handled in the app, so the owner and farmer can settle outside it. | Open |
| B11 | Does this area have warehouses, and would a small farmer use one? | Paying ₹1,500 to store 100 kg for 15 days is a lot, and many vegetables won't last that long. | Open |

## C. Being bypassed, and being called a middleman

| # | Question they will ask | Why they will ask it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | After the first order, what stops the farmer and buyer dealing directly? | The app shows both phone numbers on orders. Hotels have the most reason to do this. | Open |
| C2 | What stops a delivery partner doing the same? | They meet both sides, and also get the phone numbers. | Open |
| C3 | You take a % and a share of delivery. How are you not a middleman? | We need a sharp answer. Later fee increases would make it harder. | Open |
| C4 | Why can't a farmer sell outside your ±20% band? | If there's a glut, a farmer may need to sell lower. We would be controlling their price. | Open |

## D. Delivery and the buyer's basket

| # | Question they will ask | Why they will ask it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Why pay ₹316 for ₹200 of vegetables? | 5 items at ₹40/kg from 5 farmers makes 5 orders. ₹200 + ₹6 (3%) + ₹110 delivery (₹22 × 5) = **₹316**, which is 58% extra. With only a few farmers per area, one farmer can't fill a basket. | Open |
| D2 | Why is a 1 km trip the same price as 15 km? | The fee counts only kg. The app has no distance, only regions. | Open |
| D3 | Why would a partner join before there are orders? And can they live on this? | Fixed routes need volume we won't have at the start. | Open |
| D4 | How does produce stay fresh in heat and rain? | No crates, packing or cold transport are planned. | Open |
| D5 | Who weighs the produce? | The farmer says 20 kg, the buyer says 18 kg. Partners have no scale and there's no rule. | Open |
| D6 | The buyer isn't home or refuses the order. Then what? | Not decided. The produce is already out, and nobody says who pays. | Open |
| D7 | The farmer never answers, or no partner accepts. How long does the buyer wait? | The app has no time limit. An order can stay stuck, and with online payment the money stays held. | Open |
| D8 | When is it harvested? | For a 7 am delivery, does the farmer harvest at night, before knowing what sold? | Open |
| D9 | Can vehicles reach the farms? | Hill roads, and the farmer may be away in the field at pickup. | Open |

## E. Farmers without smartphones: what is still unsolved

| # | Question they will ask | Why they will ask it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | How does a keypad-phone farmer see and accept an order? | The app needs the farmer to press Accept, and notices are in-app only. SMS is "later", yet these farmers can't work without it. | Open |
| E2 | How do you prove a coordinator really made a visit? | Paying per visit rewards more visits, including fake ones. | Open |
| E3 | Why would anyone be a coordinator for 4 or 5 farmers? | The income may be too small. And a pilot of 10 to 20 farmers then needs 3 or 4 coordinators. | Open |
| E4 | What if the farmer just tells the helper every OTP? | Elderly farmers may hand over codes without reading them. Then the OTP protects nothing. | Open |
| E5 | The farmer has no bank account or UPI, or needs cash today. Then what? | The whole payment plan depends on a bank account, and on money after delivery. | Open |
| E6 | The crop fails but the standing listing keeps selling. Then what? | Repeating listings promise produce that may not exist. | Open |
| E7 | Can the farmer's side work on a phone today? | The website is laptop-first, the phone app isn't built, and the planned build starts with buyers. The Malayalam still needs a native check. | Open |

## H. Legal

Not legal advice. Each one needs a chartered accountant or lawyer to confirm.

| # | Question they will ask | Why they will ask it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Is "the farmer pays ₹0" legally true? | Online platforms can have to deduct TDS (Section 194-O) and collect GST TCS. We don't yet know what applies to farmers selling their own produce. | Open |
| H2 | Are you registered for GST and FSSAI? | Sources say platforms need GST registration whatever their turnover, and an FSSAI Central Licence as an e-commerce food business. | Open |
| H3 | Are you allowed to use Aadhaar? | Private companies must apply and justify it under the 2025 rules, and the rules are disputed. | Open |
| H4 | Where do you keep farmers' documents, and who can see them? | Aadhaar and land papers are sensitive. The DPDP rules apply, and uploads currently sit on one server's disk. | Open |
| H5 | Is there a registered company? Who owns the code? | It began as a college project with two teammates and now involves a friend's startup. Investors check this first. | Open |
| H6 | A partner has an accident while delivering. Who is responsible? | Insurance and gig worker rules aren't considered. | Open |
| H7 | Who legally holds the buyer's money? | Only a licensed payment company should. Refunds after the split also need a rule. | Open |
| H8 | Is the name AgriChain safe? | Companies abroad use the name. "Chain" also suggests blockchain, which we don't use. An Indian trademark search is needed. | Open |
| H9 | Are you allowed to copy prices from that website? | Its terms of use have not been checked. | Open |

## Checked against the code (2026-11-05)

- Sign-up takes any valid-looking phone number with no OTP, and any role: `backendft/src/modules/auth/auth.schemas.js`.
- Orders include farmer, buyer and partner phone numbers: `backendft/src/modules/orders/orders.service.js`.
- The only scheduled job is the daily price fetch, so nothing times out stuck orders: `backendft/src/jobs/`.
- The price is the wholesale column for Kerala from vegetablemarketprice.com: `backendft/src/modules/produce/marketPrice.scraper.js`.

## Outside sources, and how far to trust them

Quick searches, not full research. Check again before quoting any of these in the room.

- **Gateway charge:** [Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-payment-gateway-pricing-explained/) gives 2% plus 18% GST. The split (Route) fee and whether money can be held were **not confirmed**, so B1 may be lower still.
- **Local vegetable supply:** [22% in 2012](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/22/07/2015/kerala-vegetable-imports-fall-as-local-output-soars), [about 30% in 2015-16](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/news/news-amp.php?id=501308), [a ministerial claim of 75%](https://www.moneylife.in/article/kitchen-gardens-cut-down-vegetable-imports-in-kerala/42863.html). These conflict and are dated.
- **Government buying:** [VFPCK and Horticorp procurement](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/kerala/general/vegetables-will-be-procured-directly-from-farmers-and-brought-to-market-to-counter-price-hike-minister-prasad-1331804).
- **Kerala competitor:** [FarmersFZ](https://yourstory.com/2021/04/the-turning-point-agritech-startup-farmersfz-fair-price-produce). Whether it's still active is not confirmed.
- **Startup losses:** [WayCool](https://entrackr.com/?p=159541), [Ninjacart and DeHaat](https://entrackr.com/?p=162824), [Otipy](https://inc42.com/features/otipy-raised-millions-but-couldnt-beat-the-quick-commerce-wave). Figures vary by year and source.
- **Aadhaar for private companies:** [2025 rules](https://scroll.in/latest/1078654/it-ministry-notifies-rules-allowing-private-entities-to-carry-out-aadhaar-authentication), [the objections](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-private-companies-aadhaar-authentication/).
- **Tax and licences:** [GST for platforms](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/gst-compliance-licensing-ecommerce-operators), [Section 194-O](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/tds-obligations-e-commerce-operators-section-194o.html), [FSSAI for e-commerce](https://www.vjmglobal.com/blog/guideline-how-obtain-fssai-licenses-ecommerce-food-sellers-operators). Secondary sources. **No rule specific to farm produce was found.**
- **The name:** [US AGRICHAIN mark](https://trademarks.justia.com/877/88/agrichain-87788342.html), [Australian company](https://craft.co/agrichain), [Ukrainian company](https://itukraine.org.ua/?p=20429). India was not checked.
